Section 1

Getting Started & Logging In

The admin portal is the back-office control panel for the Elite Collection online store and point-of-sale system. It is separate from the public website — staff use it to manage products, orders, customers, and site content.

1.1 — Web address

Open your browser and go to:

https://admin.elitecollections.qa/

Bookmark this page. There is no app to download — the portal works in any modern browser (Chrome, Safari, Edge, Firefox) on desktop, tablet, or phone.

1.2 — Logging in

Screenshot placeholder: login.pngSign-in screen — email + password fields on a dark green background with the Elite logo
  1. Go to the web address above. You'll land on Sign in to your workspace.
  2. Enter your work email (the one your manager invited you with).
  3. Enter your password.
  4. Click Sign in. You'll be taken straight to the Dashboard.

Forgot your password?

  1. On the sign-in screen, click Forgot password?
  2. Enter your email address and submit.
  3. For security, the portal always shows a generic "check your inbox" message — this does not confirm whether the email is registered.
  4. Open the reset link from your email, choose a new password (at least 8 characters), confirm it, and submit. You'll be sent back to the sign-in screen to log in with the new password.

Accepting a staff invitation (first-time login)

If you're a new hire, you won't create your own account — an Owner or Admin invites you (see Section 16.2, Team Members). You'll receive a one-time invite link that:

  • Is valid for 48 hours — ask to be re-invited if it expires.
  • Can only be used once.
  • Opens a short form where you set your name and choose your own password, then signs you straight in.
Session security If your session expires or you're signed out, the portal automatically returns you to the sign-in screen and brings you back to the exact page you were on afterward — you won't lose your place.

1.3 — Finding your way around

Every page shares the same layout:

  • Left sidebar — the main menu (Dashboard, Catalog, Collections, Media, Storefront, Point of Sale, Orders, Customers, Feedback, Policies, Analytics, Reconciliation, Reference Lists, Settings). The sidebar only shows links your role can actually open — see Section 2 for exactly what each role sees. Click the arrow icon at the top of the sidebar to collapse it to icons-only.
  • Top bar — quick search, language switch (EN/AR), notification bell, and your account avatar (top right) for signing out.
  • On a phone, the sidebar becomes a bottom tab bar (Dashboard · Catalog · Orders · Customers · More) — tap More for the rest of the menu.
Section 2

Staff Roles & Permissions — What Each Role Can Actually Do

Every staff account is assigned one of five roles when they're invited: Owner, Admin, Manager, Cashier, or Viewer. Roles are set and changed under Settings → Team Members (Owners and Admins only — see Section 16.2).

The sidebar now matches your actual access The menu only shows links your role can open — if you don't see "Settings" or "Point of Sale" in your sidebar, your role genuinely can't use them, not a display glitch. Typing a restricted page's address directly still bounces you back to the Dashboard with no error message, but that's now a backstop for direct links, not something you'll hit from normal clicking around.

2.0 — What each role actually is

  • Owner — full access to everything, with no exceptions. The only role that can grant the Owner role to someone else, and the only role that can change the two most sensitive POS approval settings (self-close shift, self-approval — see Section 16.1b). A shop can have more than one Owner (see 2.1a below) — it isn't limited to a single founder account.
  • Admin — same day-to-day reach as Owner (catalog, storefront, orders, customers, POS, reconciliation, reports, settings, team management, devices) with one carve-out: an Admin cannot grant the Owner role to anyone, including themselves, and cannot touch the Owner-only approval settings mentioned above. Best for a store manager or operations lead who runs the business day-to-day.
  • Manager — full POS access plus reconciliation and reports, but no Settings, no Team Members, no Reference Lists. Their defining job in the system is approving Cashier actions with their manager PIN (voids, refunds, drawer opens, paid-outs, shift closes) — see Section 8.3. Best for a shift lead or senior floor staff.
  • Cashier — POS only. Can sell, park/resume a sale, reprint a receipt, and manage their own shift; anything sensitive (void, refund, no-sale drawer open, cash-out) needs a Manager/Admin/Owner PIN. Best for front-of-house till staff.
  • Viewer — read access to the general admin pages, no POS access at all. Best for a stakeholder or advisor who needs visibility, not day-to-day control.

2.1a — More than one Owner

A shop is not limited to a single Owner account. Any existing Owner can hand the Owner role to another team member directly — there is no separate "promote" step or confirmation flow: it's the same role dropdown used for every other role change (Settings → Team Members, or the Role field on the invite form), it just happens to include "Owner" as one of the choices. Two things to know:

  • Only an Owner can grant Owner. The "Owner" option in the role dropdown only appears when you yourself are signed in as an Owner — an Admin's dropdown never shows it, even to promote themselves, and a direct attempt to do so is rejected by the server, not just hidden in the UI.
  • Once someone is made Owner, their row locks — same as any Owner account, there's no role dropdown or Remove button on their row in Team Members. There is currently no built-in way to demote or remove an Owner through the portal; treat granting Owner as a permanent decision.
Manager PIN — who can hold one and why it matters Only Owner, Admin, and Manager accounts can have a manager PIN at all — Cashiers and Viewers are excluded at the database level, not just the UI. This PIN is what approves refunds, voids, drawer opens, paid-outs, and shift closes in POS (see Section 8.4). A shop with only one Owner/Admin/Manager account will find that account's own PIN doesn't work for its own approvals — this is deliberate (self-approval is blocked by design), not a bug. You need at least two people who can hold a manager PIN for POS approvals to work day-to-day.
Cashier and Viewer "read-only" note There is currently no enforced read-only mode anywhere in the portal — a Viewer who opens Catalog, for example, sees the exact same New Product / Import / Export / Edit / Delete controls an Owner would; nothing there is actually disabled for them. Treat role assignment as a trust boundary based on training and instructions for the general admin pages, not a hard technical guarantee, until read-only enforcement is built. POS, Settings, Reconciliation, and Reference Lists are the areas that are genuinely, technically restricted — those are the ones a Viewer or Cashier cannot reach no matter what.

2.2 — Changing someone's role or removing access

Covered in full in Section 16.2. In short: go to Settings → Team Members, use the role dropdown next to their name to change it (Owners only see "Owner" as an option to grant — see 2.1a above), or click Remove to revoke their access entirely.

Section 3

Dashboard

Sidebar → Dashboard

The Dashboard is your home screen — a live snapshot of the business. There is nothing to add, edit, or delete here; it's for viewing only.

Screenshot placeholder: dashboard.pngKPI cards (Revenue, Active Orders, New Customers, Low Stock) with the revenue chart and Top Products by Price list

3.1 — What you'll see

  • Date range pills (Today / 7D / 30D / 90D / Custom) — change the time window for every number on the page.
  • Revenue — total sales in the selected range.
  • Active Orders — orders still awaiting fulfillment.
  • New Customers — first-time buyers in range.
  • Low Stock — count of products below the low-stock threshold (set in Settings → General). Click this card to jump straight to the Catalog filtered to low-stock items.
  • Revenue chart — daily revenue trend for the selected range.
  • Top Products by Price — your highest-priced catalog items at a glance.
Section 4

Product Catalog

Sidebar → Catalog

This is where every product for sale lives — its photos, price, stock, sizes/colors (variants), description, and where it appears on the website.

Screenshot placeholder: catalog-list.pngProduct grid with search bar, status filter pills (All/Active/Hidden/Out of Stock/Low Stock), sort dropdown, grid/list toggle, Filters, Export, Import, and New Product button

4.1 — Finding a product

  • Type a name, SKU, or brand into the search bar at the top.
  • Use the filter pills — All / Active / Hidden / Out of Stock / Low Stock — to narrow the list.
  • Click Filters for advanced options: collection, brand, color (with swatch picker), price range, and how many results per page (25/50/100/All).
  • Use the sort dropdown (top left) to sort by Name, Price, Stock, or Newest.
  • Toggle between grid view (photo cards) and list view (compact rows) using the two icons next to the sort dropdown.

Add  4.2 — Adding a new product

  1. Click + New Product (top right of the Catalog page). A panel slides in from the right.
  2. Image Gallery — click Upload images, Pick from Media (choose from files already in the Media Library), or Add image URL. Drag thumbnails to reorder; the first image becomes the primary photo shown in the catalog and storefront.
  3. Product Info — fill in the Product Name (English), Arabic Name, Brand, and SKU (your internal product code).
  4. Pricing & Stock — enter the Price (QAR). If the product has no size/color variants, also enter Stock (units) directly here. (If you add variants in the next step, total stock is calculated automatically from the variants instead.)
  5. Variants (optional but recommended for shoes/clothing) — click Add variant to create a size/color combination, each with its own SKU, price, stock, and photo. See 4.5 — Working with variants below.
  6. Description — four separate pieces of copy, each in English and Arabic. They are not interchangeable; see 4.2a for which goes where.
  7. Organization — tick the Collections this product belongs to (e.g. Men, Kids, Sunglasses), and optionally pick related products to show under "You May Also Like" on the product page.
  8. SEO — set the Meta Title, Meta Description (160-character limit shown live), and URL Slug (the web address for this product's page).
  9. By default a new product is marked Hidden (the red "Hidden from storefront" banner and toggle at the top). Flip the toggle to make it visible to shoppers once you're happy with it.
  10. A green save bar appears at the bottom once you've made changes — click Save to publish your edits, or Discard to cancel.

4.2a — The four description fields, and where each one shows up

The old single long description was split into four purpose-built fields. Each one appears in a different place on the website, so putting the right text in the right box is what makes the product page read well.

FieldLengthWhere the shopper sees it
HookOne lineThe home-page hero and other compact places. A tagline, not a sentence about features. Example: “Full-grain leather, hand stitched in Italy.”

The old long Description is now read-only Products saved before this change keep their original long description and it still shows on the storefront, but there is no longer a box to edit it. To update that copy, move it into the four fields above. Once you do, the old text stops being used.

Edit  4.3 — Editing an existing product

  1. Click any product card (or row, in list view) to open the same slide-in panel pre-filled with its details.
  2. Change whatever you need — photos, name, price, stock, variants, description, collections, or SEO fields.
  3. Use the ‹ › arrows at the top of the panel to jump to the previous/next product without closing the panel.
  4. Click Save on the green save bar when done.
  5. To hide a product from the storefront without deleting it (e.g. temporarily out of stock, discontinuing soon), toggle Hidden at the top of the panel and save. Existing orders and shared links keep working even when hidden.

Remove  4.4 — Deleting a product

  1. Open the product's panel and scroll all the way to the bottom to Danger Zone.
  2. Click Delete product. This removes the product from the storefront and every collection.
  3. Past orders that included this product are preserved — deleting a product never affects historical order records.
Deleting is permanent There is no undo for product deletion. If you just want to stop selling something temporarily, use the Hidden toggle instead (Section 4.3) rather than deleting it.

Removing several products at once

  1. Click Select in the Catalog toolbar.
  2. Tick the products you want to act on, or click Select All.
  3. Use Set Status to hide/show many products together, or Delete to remove them all at once (with a confirmation prompt).

Duplicating a product

To create a near-identical new product (e.g. same shoe in a new colorway), open the product and use Duplicate. The copy is created hidden, with an auto-generated SKU, so you can edit it safely before publishing.

4.5 — Working with variants (sizes, colors, materials)

Variants let one product ("Croco Sandal") have multiple sellable versions ("Croco Sandal, size 42, Black"), each tracked separately for stock and price.

  1. Inside a product's panel, go to the Variants section and click Add variant, or use the Generate sizes wizard to create a full size run at once from one of your saved Size Charts.
  2. For each variant row, set: Color, Size, Stock, Price, and SKU — these five columns are always visible.
  3. Click the small ⌄ arrow on a row to expand extra fields: Material, Barcode (defaults to the SKU if left blank), Cost price, an auto-calculated Margin (colour-coded: green ≥40%, amber 20–40%, red <20%), and a Size note in English and Arabic.
  4. The Size note shows on the storefront only when a shopper picks that particular size — use it for a detail this size has and the others don't (“Back zipper”), instead of adding a whole extra photo. It stacks underneath the product-wide note from 4.2a rather than replacing it, so both can be true at once.
  5. To control which photo shows for each color, click the small photo icon in a variant's row — an image picker pops up so you can link that color to a specific gallery image.
  6. Stock turns red when a variant hits 0, and amber when below 5 — a quick visual cue to reorder stock.
  7. Use Print barcode label on a single row, or Print Labels at the bottom of the Variants section to print labels for every variant at once.

4.6 — Import, export & bulk stock updates

  • Export — downloads a CSV of the current filtered list (SKU, Name, Brand, Price, Stock, Status, Variants) — handy for stock counts or sharing with suppliers.
  • Import — upload a spreadsheet to create or update many products at once. Includes a Dry-Run preview mode (checks the file without saving), a way to Retry Failed rows, and an Import History log of past imports.
Section 5

Collections

Sidebar → Collections

Collections group products together for the storefront — e.g. "Men", "Kids", "Sunglasses". Collections can have sub-collections nested underneath them (e.g. "Men" → "Black Edition", "Exotic", "classic").

Screenshot placeholder: collections-list.pngCollections with sub-collection chips (Men → Mixed Leather, Black Edition, Exotic…) and standalone collection cards (Kids, Sunglasses, All Products)

Add  5.1 — Creating a collection

  1. Click + New Collection (top right).
  2. Toggle Visible on or off — visible collections appear on the live storefront immediately.
  3. Enter the Collection Name and an optional Description (shown on the storefront collection page).
  4. Upload or paste a URL for the Cover Image.
  5. Under Organization, optionally choose a Parent Collection to nest this one underneath as a sub-collection.
  6. Check the URL Handle field — this becomes the web address, shown as a live preview (/collection/your-handle).
  7. Save your changes from the green save bar.

Adding a sub-collection quickly

From the main Collections list, click + Add sub-collection directly under any parent collection's chip row — this pre-fills the parent for you.

Edit  5.2 — Editing a collection & managing its products

  1. Click a collection card (or its chip, for a sub-collection) to open its panel.
  2. Update name, description, cover image, visibility, or parent as needed.
  3. In Manage Products, switch between grid and list view. In grid view, drag product cards to reorder them; in list view, use the drag handles or the ↑ / ↓ buttons — this order controls how products appear on the storefront collection page.
  4. Save when done.

Remove  5.3 — Deleting a collection

Open the collection's panel and use the delete option in its Danger Zone / options menu. Deleting a collection does not delete the products inside it — it only removes the grouping.

Media Library

Sidebar → Media

A central library of every image (and 3D model) used across the catalog and storefront, with counters for total files, how many are linked to a product, how many are unlinked, and total storage used.

Screenshot placeholder: media-library.pngUpload drop-zone, filter tabs (All/Images/3D Models/Unlinked), and a grid of thumbnails each tagged Linked/Unlinked

Add  6.1 — Uploading images

  1. Drag files directly onto the Drop files to upload area, or click Browse Files to pick from your computer. Accepted: JPG, PNG, WebP, and 3D models (.glb/.gltf), up to 50MB each.
  2. To pull files straight from a shared drive, click Google Drive and paste a file or folder link.
  3. Click Auto-Link by SKU to automatically connect uploaded images to matching products, using the filename or folder name as the SKU.

Edit  6.2 — Linking & organizing files

  • Use the All / Images / 3D Models / Unlinked tabs to filter the library.
  • Click any file to open its detail panel, where you can see which product it's linked to, or set it as the Default Fallback image used when a product has no photo of its own.

Remove  6.3 — Deleting a file

Open the file's detail panel and choose Delete. This removes it from the library entirely (and from storage) — if it was linked to a product, unlink or replace it there first so the product isn't left without a photo.

Section 8

Point of Sale (POS) — Sales, Approvals, Shifts & Cash Rules

Sidebar → Point of Sale  ·  Owner/Admin/Manager/Cashier — Viewers cannot open it

A full-screen till/cashier interface for in-person sales, opened in its own layout (no sidebar) once you're on the register screen. This section covers day-to-day selling and the manager-PIN approval rules that govern every money-sensitive action.

Screenshot placeholder: pos.pngPOS screen — search/barcode bar, available products grid on the left, live Cart with Total and Take Payment button on the right

8.0 — Choosing which till this computer is

The first time you open Point of Sale on a computer, Elite asks “Which till is this?” and shows the list of counters in your shop. Tap the one you are standing at. That is the whole setup, and Elite remembers it on that computer from then on.

Each row tells you when the till was last used, and flags Shift open with the cashier's name if someone already has a shift running on it. Check that line before tapping: taking over a till that belongs to another cashier's open drawer is what causes end-of-day cash counts to disagree.

Why this screen exists at all when you're already signed in Signing in tells Elite who you are. It cannot tell Elite which counter you are standing at, and that is what the shift, the cash drawer, and the receipt numbers are attached to. Two computers ringing sales into what Elite thinks is one drawer is what makes a Z-report impossible to reconcile.
  • One till, one computer. If the same till is picked on a second computer, the first one is signed out of it and returns to this screen. That is deliberate.
  • Adding a new till — Owners and Admins get Add a new register on the same screen. Name it after the counter it sits on so cash counts are easy to match up. Cashiers and Managers pick from the list instead.
  • Setting up a counter you're not standing at — use Use a setup token instead. An Owner or Admin generates a one-time token from Settings → Devices & Security, and it is pasted on the other machine. This is the only situation that still needs a token.

8.1 — Making a sale

  1. Open Point of Sale. The till, printer, drawer, and offline signer settings restore automatically on this computer. If you are asked which till this is, see 8.0.
  2. Find items by typing into Search (name, SKU) or scanning a barcode into the Barcode field.
  3. Tap a product to add it to the Cart on the right; for products with variants, choose the size/color first.
  4. Review the Total, then click Take payment. Qatar sales tax is not added, so there is no tax line.
  5. Leave the customer as Walk-in, or search/link/create a customer while online. For card, enter the external terminal reference after the terminal approves the payment.
  6. A receipt prints automatically if a printer is configured (see Hardware below); if not, you'll see a clear "no printer configured" message rather than a silent failure, and can print again later.

Which shop's name and address print on that receipt depends on which branch this register is assigned to, not on who's logged in — see Section 16.1a if a receipt ever shows the wrong shop.

8.2 — Parking a sale

Park sale sets the current cart aside (e.g. a customer needs to grab their wallet) so you can serve someone else; retrieve it later from Parked.

8.3 — Manager-PIN approvals: what needs one, and why

A set of "cash-out" or reversal actions in POS can't be completed by a Cashier alone — they need a second person with a manager PIN to approve, entered right there on the register screen. This is a deliberate separation-of-duties control, not a glitch.

ActionNeeds manager PIN?
Ringing up a saleNo
Cash movement — Paid InNo
Cash movement — Float AdjustNo
Cash movement — Paid OutYes
Cash movement — Safe DropYes
Cash movement — No-Sale Drawer OpenYes
Void a completed saleYes
Refund a completed sale (partial or full)Yes
Close a shift / generate a Z-reportYes

Who can approve, and the self-approval rule

  • Only Owner, Admin, and Manager accounts can hold a working manager PIN. Cashiers cannot set one and can never be the approver — only the person whose action needs approval.
  • You cannot approve your own action with your own PIN — if the person ringing up the till and the person entering the PIN are the same account, it's rejected as "Manager PIN is incorrect" even if the PIN is correct. This is intentional (approver-separation), and it means at least two Owner/Admin/Manager-capable people need working PINs for a shop to actually use void/refund/paid-out/drawer-open/Z-report day to day.
  • Enter the PIN wrong 5 times in a row and PIN verification locks for 5 minutes on that register for that cashier — this protects against PIN guessing, but means a genuine mistake pile-up (e.g. two people fumbling a handoff) can lock things out for a few minutes. Wait it out; there's no override.
  • Once a PIN is accepted, the approval is valid for 5 minutes and tied to that specific action — if you take too long between entering the PIN and finishing the action, you may need to re-approve.

Setting your own manager PIN

  • Owner / Admin — Settings → General → Manager PIN card (see Section 16.1). Enter a 4-8 digit PIN and click Save PIN.
  • Manager — use the My Manager PIN link in the sidebar (Managers can't reach the rest of Settings, so this is a dedicated page just for the PIN). Same 4-8 digit rule.
  • A cashier will simply be prompted for "Manager PIN" on the register screen when their action needs approval — they don't need to know whose PIN it is, just hand the screen to any available Owner/Admin/Manager to type it in.
Emergency self-approval exists, but only via direct database access There's a tenant-level flag that would let a single Owner/Admin/Manager approve their own actions in a genuine one-person-shop emergency. As of this writing it has no on/off switch in the portal UI — it can only be turned on by someone with direct database access. If your shop only ever has one PIN-capable person on site, raise this with whoever manages the system rather than trying to work around it in the UI.

8.4 — Returns & voids

  1. Click Returns / voids in the POS toolbar.
  2. Search for the receipt by number.
  3. To void the entire sale: enter a reason, enter a manager PIN (not your own), and click Void entire sale. Stock is automatically restocked.
  4. To refund some or all of the items: adjust the Qty field for the line(s) being returned (leave the "Restock" box checked unless the item is damaged/not sellable), enter a reason and manager PIN, then click Refund selected. You can refund a sale in more than one pass — the receipt shows how many units are still refundable each time you look it up.
  5. Use Reprint receipt in the same lookup panel to reprint any past sale's receipt. A completed void also prints a clearly marked VOID customer copy.

8.5 — Cash movements

Click Shift report → Cash movement to log money going in or out of the drawer outside of a sale.

  1. Choose the Type: Paid in, Paid out, Safe drop, Float adjust, or No-sale drawer open.
  2. For Paid in / Paid out / Safe drop / Float adjust, enter the Amount.
  3. Always enter a Reason — it's required and shows up in the shift's cash-movement log and the Z-report.
  4. For Paid out, Safe drop, and No-sale drawer open, enter a Manager PIN (see 8.3) before clicking Record movement.

8.6 — Shifts & the Z-report

  1. At the start of the day, sign in and open POS. Confirm the readiness card shows the printer and offline signer as ready; use Retry if needed.
  2. If there is no shift, count and enter the Opening Cash, then click Open shift. No sale can be posted without an open shift.
  3. If POS shows Resolve the previous shift, do not bypass it. A previous-day shift, a shift owned by another cashier, or a shift still closing must be reviewed and closed with the manager/Z-report flow before today's cashier sells.
  4. During the shift, click Shift report at any time to see a live snapshot: Gross, Refunds, Voids, Net, Cash In, Cash Out, Expected Cash, and the Transactions count.
  5. At the end of the shift, count the actual cash in the drawer, enter it under Physical Cash, enter a manager PIN, and click Create Z report and close. This is the one action even an Owner/Admin cannot self-approve — a second person's PIN is always required to close a shift.
  6. Closing a shift is final for that shift — a new one starts fresh from Open a cashier shift next time someone rings anything up.

Every closed Z-report is kept in Z-report history (from the same Shift report panel), where you can Reprint it (needs a configured printer) or Export CSV for the whole history — see Section 17 for a full map of every export/report location in the portal.

8.7 — Hardware

Click Hardware in the POS top bar to configure the QZ receipt printer, offline signer, and cash drawer for this device. Configure each physical register once; Elite saves these settings in that browser profile and reconnects QZ automatically after sign-in, reload, or Windows restart.

Setting up a brand-new register? One more step, in Settings Hardware here controls how this device prints. It does not control which shop's address it prints — that's a separate, one-time step: assign the register to a branch under Settings → Devices & Security (Section 16.1a). Skip it and the register still prints fine, just for whichever branch happens to be the account's default.
  • QZ Tray and the Elite device signer must start with Windows. The signer health address is http://127.0.0.1:8182/health.
  • Never use private/incognito mode and never clear Elite's cookies/site data on a register. That wipes the hardware settings, the catalog cache, and any offline sales still waiting to sync — those cannot be recovered. Which till the computer is stays easy to fix: just pick it again from the list (8.0).
  • A printer failure never reverses a saved sale. Find the transaction and reprint; do not ring the sale again.

8.8 — Offline operation

  • The POS can continue from its cached catalog and reserved receipt numbers when the API is unreachable, even if Windows still says Wi-Fi is connected.
  • At 8 hours without a fresh catalog, POS warns the cashier. At 12 hours it blocks offline payment until the register reconnects and refreshes.
  • Watch the queue strip. When connectivity returns, queued sales synchronize automatically and the count must return to zero exactly once.
  • Do not close a shift while sales are pending/rejected, and never clear browser data to remove a queue warning.

8.9 — Shared stock across both shops

One inventory pool Both shops, the stock room, and the website share the same quantity. A sale anywhere reduces that shared total. Bringing merchandise from the stock room to a shop, or moving it between shops, is a physical replenishment only — do not record it as a sale, transfer, or stock adjustment. The current system shows whether an item exists anywhere in the business; the team accepts that it does not show which building holds it.
Section 9

Card Settlement Reconciliation

Sidebar → Reconciliation  ·  Owner/Admin/Manager only

A back-office tool for closing the books on card payments each day: you enter what the bank's settlement export says landed for a register/day, the system compares it against what the POS itself recorded for card sales that day, and flags anything that doesn't line up.

Screenshot placeholder: reconciliation.pngEnter Settlement card (Register, Business Date, Bank Settlement Total, Check POS total, Submit settlement) above a Reconciliation History table with a status filter

9.1 — Entering a daily settlement

  1. Choose the Register and Business Date you're reconciling.
  2. Click Check POS total to pull the live total the POS recorded for card sales on that register/day — do this first so you have both numbers in front of you.
  3. Enter the Bank Settlement Total exactly as shown on the bank's export for that day.
  4. Click Submit settlement.

9.2 — Matched vs. exception

  • If the bank total and the POS total are within QAR 1.00 of each other, the entry is automatically marked Matched — no further action needed.
  • If they differ by more than QAR 1.00, it's flagged an Exception. Exceptions stay visible in Reconciliation History until resolved.
  • To resolve an exception, you must add a manager note explaining the discrepancy (e.g. "terminal double-charged, refunded next day") — the system will not let you mark it Resolved without one, and this is enforced on the server, not just the form.

9.3 — Reconciliation History

One row per register per business day, filterable by status (All / Pending / Matched / Exception / Resolved). This is your audit trail for "does our card money actually match what the bank paid us" over time.

Bulk CSV import isn't available yet Entries are added one register/day at a time by hand. If your bank's export covers many days at once, you'll need to enter each day separately for now.

16.1 — General

Screenshot placeholder: settings-general.pngStore Information card, Branches card, Manager PIN card, and POS Registers card, stacked top to bottom
  • Store Information — Store Name, Currency (QAR — Qatari Riyal), Timezone (Asia/Qatar), Default Language, and the Low Stock Threshold — the stock number below which products are flagged "Low Stock" across the Catalog and Dashboard.
  • Logo — click Edit to replace the store logo shown in the sidebar and on printed receipts.
  • Branches — one printable receipt identity (bilingual Trade Name, Address, Phone, CR/License Number, Return Policy) per physical shop, required for compliance in Qatar. Since Elite runs more than one shop, this replaced a single shared profile — see Section 16.1a below for the full walkthrough, it matters for every new register you connect.
  • Manager PIN — where Owner and Admin accounts set the 4-8 digit PIN used to approve POS actions (void, refund, drawer-open, paid-out, shift close). Enter a new PIN and click Save PIN. See Section 8.3 for the full approval rules, including the self-approval block.

16.1a — Branches: one receipt identity per shop

Every POS receipt has to say which shop it came from — the printed trade name, address, phone, CR number and return policy. With two physical shops, one shared address is wrong for whichever shop isn't printing it, so each shop gets its own branch here, and every physical register is assigned to exactly one of them.

Screenshot placeholder: settings-branches.pngList of branch cards (name + "Default" badge + Set as default/Edit/Delete), an "Add branch" button, and the expanded edit form showing trade name, address, phone, CR number and return policy fields

Add  Creating a branch

  1. Click Add branch above the branch list.
  2. Enter a Branch name — this is an internal label only (e.g. "The Pearl", "Lusail"), so you and your team can tell branches apart in this list and in the register-assignment dropdown. It is never printed on a receipt.
  3. Fill in the fields that are printed: Trade Name, Address (multi-line — type each line the way it should appear on paper), and Phone are required. CR/License Number and Return Policy are optional but expected for compliance — confirm exact wording with your accountant.
  4. Click Save.

Update  Editing a branch, or changing which one is default

  • Click Edit on a branch's card to change any of its fields, then Save.
  • Click Set as default on any non-default branch to make it the fallback branch for this account. See the callout below for exactly what "default" controls.

Remove  Deleting a branch

Click the trash icon on a branch's card. Two things can block this, both on purpose:

  • You can't delete a branch while any register is still assigned to it — reassign those registers to a different branch first (see below), then delete.
  • You can't delete your only remaining branch — add a second one before removing the first, if you're replacing rather than adding.

Assigning a register to a branch

This is the step that actually connects a physical till to a shop, and it's easy to forget when setting up a new register.

  1. Go to Settings → Devices & Security (Section 16.1b, below).
  2. Find the register in the table and use the Branch dropdown on its row to pick the correct shop.
  3. The change is saved immediately — no separate Save button. Give it a few minutes: the register caches this for up to 5 minutes, so a receipt printed right after reassigning may still show the old branch briefly.
An unassigned register prints the default branch — silently A register that has never been assigned to a branch does not fail or show a blank receipt. It prints whichever branch is currently set as Default, which is a completely normal-looking receipt — just possibly for the wrong shop. When setting up a new till for a specific shop, always confirm by reading the address on an actual test receipt from that exact register, not by assuming the Settings screen alone proves it's correct.

16.1b — Devices & Security

Owner/Admin only — this whole tab is hidden from Manager, Cashier and Viewer accounts. It's where connected POS registers actually live, separate from the "generate a setup code" step in Section 16.1 above.

  • Registered Devices — every register that has completed setup, with its status, Branch assignment (this is where you do the assignment described above), and last-seen time. Click Revoke on a register that's been decommissioned or lost — this signs it out immediately and it can no longer open shifts or take payments; it does not un-print anything already sold. A revoked till also disappears from the “Which till is this?” list, so nobody can pick it by mistake.
  • Enrollment Tokens — setup codes that haven't been used yet (or are still within their 15-minute window). Revoke one here if it was sent to the wrong place. Most tills never need a token now: staff pick their counter from a list instead (Section 8.0), and tokens are only for setting up a machine nobody is standing at.
  • Manager PINs — a list of which Owner/Admin/Manager accounts currently have a working PIN set (see Section 8.3 for what PINs are used for). Reset someone's PIN from here if they've forgotten it or left the shop.
  • Self-close shift policy — Owner only. Toggles whether the cashier who opened a shift can also close it themselves, or whether a second manager must approve the close (see Section 8.6).

16.2 — Team Members

Screenshot placeholder: settings-team-members.pngInvite Team Member form (Email + Role, with a live description of what that role can do) and the Team Members table with role dropdowns and Remove buttons

17.1 — Stocktake

  1. Owner/Admin opens Stocktake, enters a reference, and chooses blind count (expected quantity hidden while counting) or open count.
  2. Count each variant and submit. If two counters disagree, recount and explicitly resolve the discrepancy before posting.
  3. Posting applies only the counted discrepancy against current stock, so sales made during the count are preserved. A posted stocktake is immutable and remains in history.
Use adjustments only for a real quantity change Damage, correction, found stock, or another approved reason changes the shared inventory and must be recorded. Moving the same units between the stock room and either shop does not change the shared total and must not be entered as an adjustment.

17.2 — Diagnostics

Owner/Admin can use Diagnostics to investigate without opening developer tools. Errors are grouped by source (server, POS client, admin client, or browser security policy), show occurrence count, status, register/request context, and can be marked resolved. The Audit tab searches actions by request ID and date.

  1. Ask the cashier for the short reference shown with the error and note the exact time/register.
  2. Open Diagnostics → Errors, search the reference, and open the matching group for context/stack details.
  3. Use the full Request ID in the Audit tab to reconstruct related server actions. Mark resolved only after the problem is understood or corrected.
No single combined report yet Analytics → Expenses & Net Profit now gives a true Revenue − Cost of Goods Sold − Operating Expenses figure for a chosen period (see Section 14.2), but there is still no single page that also folds in inventory movement, cash reconciliation, and card settlement — you assemble that fuller picture from the Dashboard, Analytics, Z-report history, and Reconciliation history separately.
Section 18

Expenses — Operating Cost Ledger

Sidebar → Expenses  ·  Owner/Admin only

The running-costs ledger — rent, salaries, utilities, marketing, and everything else that leaves the business but isn't the cost of a product itself. This is what feeds the Net Profit figure on Analytics → Expenses & Net Profit (Section 14.2); with nothing logged here, Net Profit is only Revenue minus Cost of Goods Sold.

Screenshot placeholder: expenses.pngDate-range and category filters, a totals strip (grand total + top 3 categories), the expense ledger table with category pills, and the + Add Expense button top right
Do not log inbound shipping here The shipping cost you pay to bring stock in is already part of each product's cost (Cost + Shipping on the variant, see Section 4.5) and is already subtracted automatically as part of Cost of Goods Sold. Logging that same shipping again under the Logistics category here would subtract it from profit twice. The Logistics category is for delivering orders to customers, not for bringing stock in — the Expense drawer shows this same warning the moment you pick that category.

Add  18.1 — Logging an expense

  1. Click + Add Expense (top right of the Expenses page). A panel slides in from the right.
  2. Enter the Amount (QAR), the Date, and choose a Category — Rent, Salaries, Utilities, Marketing, Logistics, Supplies, Software, Fees, Maintenance, or Other.
  3. Optionally fill in Vendor (who was paid), Payment Method (Cash, Card, Bank Transfer, Cheque, Other), and a Note.
  4. For a bill that repeats, set Repeats to Monthly or Yearly — see 18.2 below.
  5. Optionally attach a photo or PDF of the receipt under Receipt.
  6. Click Save Changes. The drawer closes and the new entry appears at the top of the ledger.

18.2 — Recurring expenses

A bill that repeats every month or year — rent, a software subscription — only needs to be entered once. Set Repeats to Monthly or Yearly when you first log it, and every later occurrence appears automatically in the ledger, tagged Upcoming, without you re-entering it.

  • An Upcoming row is a projection, not a real entry yet — opening and saving it (even with no changes) records that one period on its own, without touching the rest of the schedule.
  • To stop a recurring bill going forward, open its most recent real entry and change Repeats back to "Does not repeat."

Edit  18.3 — Editing or deleting an expense

Click any row in the ledger to reopen its drawer. Change any field and Save Changes, or scroll to the bottom and click Delete Expense to remove it — deleting stops it from counting toward Net Profit from that point on.

18.4 — Filtering, search & export

  • Use the From / To date fields and the Category dropdown to narrow the ledger; the totals strip updates to match.
  • Search by vendor or note text using the search box.
  • Click Export CSV to download the currently filtered list — useful for handing figures to an accountant.

18.5 — Importing POS cash-outs

Cash paid out of a POS till mid-shift (petty cash, a delivery tip) is already recorded in POS as a Cash Movement (Section 8.5), separate from this ledger. Click Import POS cash-outs at the bottom of the Expenses page to pull those paid-out movements into this ledger as expenses, categorized as Other. It's safe to click more than once — a cash movement already imported is never imported twice.

Section 20

Troubleshooting & FAQ

19.1 — Access & sign-in

I can't see Settings, Reference Lists, or Reconciliation — or they redirect me back to Dashboard.

Settings and Reference Lists are Owner/Admin only. Reconciliation is Owner/Admin/Manager only — your sidebar correctly leaves those links out if your role can't use them (see Section 2). If you follow an old bookmark or a direct link to one of these pages anyway, you'll be silently bounced back to Dashboard rather than shown an error — that's still normal, not a bug. If you need something changed there, ask an Owner, Admin, or (for Reconciliation) a Manager.

I can't open Point of Sale.

POS is available to Owner, Admin, Manager, and Cashier roles. Viewers cannot access it and are redirected to Dashboard — ask an Admin to check your assigned role under Settings → Team Members.

My invite link says it's expired, or takes me to a broken "localhost" address.

Invite links expire after 48 hours and can only be used once — ask an Owner or Admin to send a fresh invitation from Settings → Team Members. If the link itself looks broken (points to localhost:4300 instead of the real portal address), that was a known issue fixed on 2026-07-20 — if you still see it, the portal may need redeploying; flag it to whoever maintains the system.

I forgot my password.

Use Forgot password? on the sign-in screen (see Section 1.2).

19.2 — Point of Sale & manager PIN

"Manager PIN is incorrect" even though I just saved that exact PIN.

This is almost always the self-approval rule, not a wrong PIN — you cannot approve your own action with your own PIN (see Section 8.3). Hand the screen to a different Owner/Admin/Manager to enter theirs. If a second person's correct PIN is also rejected, double-check it was actually saved (Settings → General or My Manager PIN shows a "PIN saved" confirmation toast when it works) and that you're both on the same register/tenant.

PIN entry is locked / "Manager PIN verification is temporarily locked."

Five wrong PIN attempts in a row locks verification for that cashier on that register for 5 minutes. There's no override — wait it out, then try again with the correct PIN.

Only one person on shift, and they can't approve their own void/refund/Z-report.

Expected behavior — see the self-approval rule above. You need at least two Owner/Admin/Manager-capable people with working PINs for these actions. There is a tenant-level "emergency self-approval" flag that can bypass this, but it currently requires direct database access to turn on — ask whoever manages the system if your shop genuinely needs this.

A cashier says they can't set a manager PIN.

That's correct and expected — only Owner, Admin, and Manager accounts can hold a manager PIN. A Cashier can be the one requesting approval but can never be the one granting it.

I got a "Session expired" message right after a wrong PIN attempt, and the product list briefly went blank.

This was a real bug (a false session-expiry toast plus a momentary empty catalog after a rejected PIN) that was fixed on 2026-07-20 and confirmed resolved in repeated testing. If you still see it, you were not actually signed out — reload the page and continue; but please flag it, since it means the fix may have regressed.

"No receipt printer is configured" when I try to print or reprint.

This is a graceful, expected message on any device without a connected receipt printer — not a crash or data-loss. Configure the printer via the Hardware button in the POS top bar (see Section 8.7) on the physical register, or simply note that the sale/Z-report is safely saved and can be printed later once a printer is set up.

The hardware indicator is red after sign-in or restart.

Wait briefly for automatic reconnect, then click Hardware or Retry. Confirm QZ Tray is running and open http://127.0.0.1:8182/health on the register; it should return ok. Do not reconnect/enroll the register again. If support needs the local timeline, collect C:\ProgramData\ElitePOS\device-signer\logs\signer.log.

Wi-Fi says connected, but POS says offline.

This can be correct: the browser may reach the Wi-Fi router but not the Elite API. POS tests server reachability and safely falls back to its offline queue. Continue only if the catalog is within the allowed age and the queue/storage warnings are healthy; otherwise reconnect the API before taking payment.

POS says "Resolve the previous shift" in the morning.

The register still has a previous-day shift, another cashier's shift, or a shift whose close is incomplete. Use Review and close previous shift and complete the manager-controlled Z report. Do not clear browser data, and do not pick or add a different register to bypass it.

POS is asking “Which till is this?” on a computer that was already set up.

Something wiped this computer's local Elite data — usually cleared site data, private/incognito mode, a new browser profile, or a re-imaged machine. It can also mean an Owner revoked this register, or the same till was picked on another computer (only one computer can hold a till at a time). Just pick the same counter from the list again and carry on; no Owner and no setup token is needed. If the till is not in the list, it has been revoked — an Owner can check under Settings → Devices & Security (Section 16.1b).

Check for unsynced sales first Picking the till again restores the till itself, but it does not bring back offline sales that were still waiting to sync when the data was cleared. Those are gone. If this machine had been selling offline, tell an Owner before carrying on so the day's takings can be checked against the Z-report.

Two computers are fighting over the same till.

Each time one picks the till, the other gets signed out of it. That is working as intended — a till is one physical counter, and two computers sharing one drawer makes the end-of-day cash count impossible to reconcile. Give each counter its own register: an Owner or Admin uses Add a new register on the same screen (Section 8.0).

I have an error reference and need to know what happened.

Give the short reference, exact time, register, and action to an Owner/Admin. They can search Diagnostics → Errors, then use the full Request ID in Diagnostics → Audit. This correlates the cashier message with browser and server logs without exposing technical details to the cashier.

Stock counts look wrong after a void or refund.

Voids and refunds restock automatically as long as the Restock checkbox was ticked when the refund was processed (it's ticked by default — untick it only for damaged/non-sellable returns). If a number still looks off, check the product's Catalog page directly rather than relying on memory of the pre-sale count.

Stock moved from the stock room to one of the shops. What should I enter?

Nothing. Both shops, the stock room, and website use one shared inventory quantity, so moving the same unit physically does not change it. Use Stocktake or a reason-coded adjustment only when the business's total quantity is genuinely wrong, damaged, found, or otherwise changed.

19.3 — Data & content

My changes don't show on the live website.

For the Storefront Editor specifically, edits save as a draft first. Check for the "Layout Unpublished" badge — you need to click Publish Layout for shoppers to see your changes. Other sections (Catalog, Collections, Orders, etc.) save and go live immediately once you click Save.

I deleted a product by mistake.

Product deletion is permanent and cannot be undone from the portal. Recreate the product manually, or contact whoever manages your data backups if this was a serious mistake.

A customer's order history disappeared after I deleted their profile.

It shouldn't — customer deletion is a soft delete, meaning the record is hidden but not destroyed, and their order history remains intact for accounting. Ask an Admin to check whether the customer can be restored.

A product's size/color options look wrong or mixed up (e.g. "Black Edition" showing color names where sizes should be).

This is a known data-entry issue on specific products, not a system bug — it needs a manual correction in Catalog → that product → Variants, not a code fix. Flag it to whoever manages the catalog data if you spot it.

Glossary

TermMeaning
SKUStock Keeping Unit — your internal product code, unique to each product or variant.
VariantA specific sellable version of a product (a size + color combination), each with its own stock and price.
HiddenA product or collection that exists in the system but isn't shown to shoppers on the storefront.
Draft / PublishThe Storefront Editor's safety system — changes save as a draft and only go live once published.
FulfillmentThe shipping/delivery stage of an order (Awaiting, Processing, Shipped, Delivered).
Soft deleteHiding a record (like a customer) from view without permanently destroying its data.
Low Stock ThresholdThe stock number below which a product is flagged as "Low Stock" (set in Settings → General).
POSPoint of Sale — the in-store cashier/checkout screen.
Z-reportAn end-of-shift cash reconciliation summary in POS, generated when a shift is closed and kept in Z-report history afterward.
Manager PINA 4-8 digit code held by Owner/Admin/Manager accounts, entered in POS to approve void, refund, drawer-open, paid-out, and shift-close actions.
Approver-separation / self-approval blockThe rule that a manager PIN cannot approve an action performed by that same account — a second, different Owner/Admin/Manager must enter theirs.
Reconciliation (card settlement)Matching the bank's daily card settlement total against the POS's own recorded card total for a register/day; a mismatch beyond QAR 1.00 becomes an Exception.
Exception (Reconciliation)A settlement entry where the bank total and POS total differ by more than QAR 1.00 — requires a manager note to mark Resolved.
Cash movementAny recorded cash-in/cash-out in POS outside of a sale — Paid In, Paid Out, Safe Drop, Float Adjust, or No-Sale Drawer Open.
Shared stockOne inventory quantity used by both shops, the stock room, and website. Internal physical movement is not a sale, transfer, or adjustment.
Offline queueSales safely saved on the register while the API is unreachable and synchronized automatically after reconnection.
Request ID / referenceThe trace code that connects a cashier-visible error to Diagnostics, audit events, and server logs.
StocktakeA controlled physical count whose posted discrepancy corrects shared inventory without undoing sales made during counting.
Device signerThe localhost Windows service that lets QZ verify and print receipts while the Elite API is offline.
QARQatari Riyal — the store's currency.
COGS (Cost of Goods Sold)What the products sold in a period actually cost — quantity × each variant's Cost + Shipping — used to calculate Net Profit on Analytics.
Net ProfitRevenue minus Cost of Goods Sold minus Operating Expenses, shown on Analytics for the selected date range.
Recurring expenseAn expense entered once in the Expenses ledger with Repeats set to Monthly or Yearly, which then appears automatically every period after.
